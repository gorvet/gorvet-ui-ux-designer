# UX Usability

Optimize for successful task completion, not screen decoration.

## Evaluate

- Is the primary task obvious?
- Is the next action recognizable without instruction?
- Can unnecessary choices or steps be removed?
- Are frequent actions easier than rare ones?
- Does the interface prevent mistakes before explaining them?
- Are system status, loading, success and failure visible?
- Can users undo, cancel, retry, or recover where appropriate?
- Is information recognized rather than memorized across screens?
- Is complexity progressively disclosed instead of hidden unpredictably?
- Are destructive or irreversible actions proportional to their risk?

## Rules

- Familiar conventions are a usability feature unless novelty serves a real product goal.
- Do not hide high-frequency actions for visual cleanliness.
- Do not disable an action without making the reason and remedy clear.
- Prefer prevention over error messaging; prefer recovery over dead ends.
- Use sensible defaults only when likely correct, reversible, and aligned with user interests.
- Design empty, loading, error, offline/partial and success states where relevant.
- Expert workflows may need higher density and shortcuts; do not force consumer-style spaciousness on operational tools.

## Handoff

Describe the intended task flow, primary/secondary actions, feedback, prevention/recovery behavior, and any progressive disclosure. Keep aesthetic prescriptions in the visual/design-system skills.
